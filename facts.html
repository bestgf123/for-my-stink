🌎 Birthday Traditions That Are Actually Weird

🇧🇷 Brazil — Cake, but make it personal

In a Brazilian birthday tradition, the birthday person may give the first slice of cake to someone especially important to them. Which sounds sweet until you realise you're standing there having to publicly decide who your favourite person is.

🇲🇽 Mexico — Your face belongs in the cake

In Mexico, there's a tradition called la mordida where the birthday person takes the first bite of cake and may get pushed face-first into it by everyone else. So yes, the cake is technically a dessert and a trap.

🇨🇦 Canada — Grease the nose

In parts of Atlantic Canada, a birthday tradition involves sneaking butter onto the birthday person's nose. The idea is that the slippery nose makes it harder for bad luck to get hold of them. Which is apparently a perfectly normal thing to do.

🇭🇺 Hungary — Your ears are getting involved

A Hungarian birthday tradition involves pulling the birthday person's earlobes while wishing them a long life. The traditional sentiment basically wishes for their ears to eventually reach their ankles.

🇨🇳 China — Do not break the noodle

Long noodles are eaten for birthdays because they symbolise longevity. The idea is to eat the noodle without cutting or breaking it. So apparently your lifespan can be represented by one extremely inconvenient noodle.

🇩🇪 Germany — Don't wish too early

In Germany, traditionally wishing someone a happy birthday before their actual birthday is considered bad luck. So if you're the kind of person who gets annoyed when people celebrate too early, Germany has your back.

📅 September 7 — On This Day

🇧🇷 Brazil became independent

On September 7, 1822, Pedro declared Brazil's independence from Portugal. September 7 is now Brazil's Independence Day.

🗽 “Uncle Sam” appeared in print

On September 7, 1813, the nickname “Uncle Sam” was used in print in the Troy Post in connection with the War of 1812.

✈️ A very early aircraft milestone

On September 7, 1906, Alberto Santos-Dumont successfully flew his 14-bis aircraft at Bagatelle in France.

🎨 The Mona Lisa briefly caused a ridiculous mystery

In 1911, Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested on suspicion of involvement in the theft of the Mona Lisa. He hadn't actually stolen it. The painting had disappeared from the Louvre just weeks earlier.
