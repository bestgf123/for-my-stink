🌎 Birthday Traditions That Are Actually Weird

🇧🇷 Brazil — Choose your favourite

In a Brazilian birthday tradition, the birthday person may give the first slice of cake to someone especially important to them.

Which sounds lovely until you're suddenly standing there having to publicly decide who your favourite person is.

🇲🇽 Mexico — The cake fights back

In Mexico, there's a tradition called la mordida, where the birthday person takes the first bite of cake and may then be pushed face-first into it.

So the cake is both dessert and an ambush.

🇨🇦 Canada — Weaponised butter

In parts of Atlantic Canada, a birthday tradition involves putting butter on the birthday person's nose.

Apparently a slippery nose makes it harder for bad luck to get hold of you. This is somehow considered a reasonable solution.

🇭🇺 Hungary — PAY THE EAR TAX

A Hungarian birthday tradition involves pulling the birthday person's earlobes while wishing them a long life.

The traditional sentiment even involves wishing that their ears will eventually reach their ankles.

🇨🇳 China — One noodle. Do not break it.

Long noodles are eaten for birthdays because they symbolise longevity.

The idea is to eat the noodle without cutting or breaking it.

So apparently your lifespan can be represented by one extremely inconvenient noodle.

🇩🇰 Denmark — Prepare for the flag apocalypse

Danish birthday celebrations are often decorated with lots of Danish flags.

So if you walked into a Danish birthday party without knowing what was happening, you might reasonably assume you'd accidentally entered a very enthusiastic national celebration.

🇰🇷 South Korea — Seaweed soup birthday

In South Korea, birthday celebrations traditionally involve miyeok-guk, a seaweed soup.

It is associated with the care mothers receive after giving birth, making it one of the few birthday traditions where everyone is basically reminded that Mum did most of the work.

📅 September 7 — On This Day

🇧🇷 Brazil declared independence

On September 7, 1822, Pedro declared Brazil's independence from Portugal. September 7 is now Brazil's Independence Day.

🗽 Uncle Sam appeared in print

On September 7, 1813, the nickname "Uncle Sam" appeared in print in the Troy Post in connection with the War of 1812.

✈️ Santos-Dumont takes flight

On September 7, 1906, Brazilian aviation pioneer Alberto Santos-Dumont successfully flew his 14-bis aircraft at Bagatelle in France.

🎨 The Mona Lisa mystery gets messy

In 1911, Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested on suspicion of involvement in the theft of the Mona Lisa.

He hadn't actually stolen it. The painting had disappeared from the Louvre only weeks earlier.
