🌎 Birthday Traditions That Are Absolutely Unhinged

🇧🇷 Brazil — Choose your favourite person

The birthday person traditionally gives the first slice of cake to someone they consider especially important. Which is lovely, until you realise you have to make that decision in front of everyone.

🇲🇽 Mexico — The cake is a weapon

In Mexico, la mordida involves the birthday person taking the first bite of their cake, after which people may push their face into it. You thought you were having dessert. You were actually walking into a trap.

🇦🇷 Argentina — Pull the ribbons

At some Argentine birthday parties, guests may pull ribbons attached to a cake or other decoration. One lucky ribbon can reveal a small prize. Birthday party or extremely low-budget game show? You decide.

🇩🇰 Denmark — More flags. Obviously.

Danish birthdays are often decorated with Danish flags. Tiny flags, big flags, cake flags — apparently the correct amount of flags for a birthday is simply “more.”

🇨🇳 China — Do not break the noodle

Long noodles are eaten for birthdays because they symbolise longevity. The idea is to eat the noodle without cutting or breaking it. So apparently your lifespan can be represented by one extremely inconvenient noodle.

🇭🇺 Hungary — Your ears are getting involved

A Hungarian birthday tradition involves pulling the birthday person's earlobes while wishing them a long life. The traditional sentiment basically wishes for their ears to eventually reach their ankles.

🇰🇷 South Korea — Birthday soup

Miyeok-guk, a seaweed soup, is traditionally eaten on birthdays in South Korea. It is connected with the soup mothers eat after giving birth, so it is also a little nod to your mum. Unexpectedly wholesome. Still soup, though.

📅 September 7 — On This Day

🇧🇷 Brazil became independent

On September 7, 1822, Pedro declared Brazil's independence from Portugal. September 7 is now Brazil's Independence Day.

🗽 “Uncle Sam” appeared in print

On September 7, 1813, the nickname “Uncle Sam” was used in print in the Troy Post in connection with the War of 1812.

✈️ An early aircraft milestone

On September 7, 1906, Alberto Santos-Dumont successfully flew his 14-bis aircraft at Bagatelle in France.

🎨 The Mona Lisa mystery

In 1911, Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested on suspicion of involvement in the theft of the Mona Lisa. He hadn't actually stolen it. The painting had disappeared from the Louvre just weeks earlier.
